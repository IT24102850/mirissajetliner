Why Whale Watching in Mirissa?

Mirissa is one of the world’s best places to see large whales — especially blue whales — thanks to deep feeding grounds nearby. Our tours combine expert guides, specialist boat design and a strong conservation approach to ensure great sightings with minimal disturbance.

Top Reasons to Choose Us

  • High sighting probability (seasonal): blue whales, sperm whales, dolphins.
  • Experienced crew & marine guides with local knowledge.
  • Upper-deck seating for unobstructed viewing and photography.
  • Comfortable vessel, life jackets, refreshments and insurance.

Best Time to Visit

Blue whales and many large baleen species are most commonly seen between November and April. Sperm whales and other species are often observed year-round in certain locations — we advise early morning departures for calmer seas and optimal sightings.

What to Expect on a Tour

  1. Departure from Mirissa Harbour at the scheduled time (usually 6:30 AM).
  2. Short safety briefing and distribution of life jackets.
  3. Search and slow-approach to sightings following WDCS guidelines.
  4. Opportunity for photography, onboard commentary and spotting several species.
  5. Return to harbour with light refreshments served.

Duration: Typically 3–5 hours. Bring sunscreen, hat, motion-sickness remedies and a camera.

Safety & Conservation

We follow international whale watching guidelines: maintaining safe distances, minimizing pursuit, and avoiding loud noise. Safety briefings and life jackets are provided to all passengers. Our crew are trained in first aid and emergency procedures.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are sightings guaranteed?

Sightings cannot be guaranteed — wildlife is unpredictable — but Mirissa has a high seasonal sighting rate for blue whales. We provide advice on best seasons and offer rebooking options in case of cancellations for weather or safety.

What should I bring?

Sunscreen, hat, light jacket, camera, seasickness medication (if needed), and water. We provide light refreshments on most trips.

How long is the tour?

3–5 hours depending on sightings and sea conditions.
